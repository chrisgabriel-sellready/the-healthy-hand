Type

Detox, honestly

Headlines: Bebas Neue, all caps, the font across the storefront. In email, the fallback is Impact.

One cup at night, two capsules in the morning. A small ritual makes a healthier week easier to keep.

Body: Open Sans, which the site already loads. 16 to 18px, left-aligned.

Balance due: 1 good reset

Courier Prime only for borrowed formats like the receipt.

Logo

On white or grey at 70 to 80px wide in email. On coral or black, put it in a white tile: the red "HEALTHY" disappears on coral. We only have the full-color version, so ask for a one-color white logo.

Buttons

Shop the sale

Lime with Bebas caps, tracked wide, like the site's "Sign up" and "Shop now." The site puts white text on lime, which is hard to read (about 2:1 contrast), so emails use black text on the same lime.

Signature moves

Join our list

Two-tone headlineBlack, then the key words in coral. It's straight from the site footer ("Join our mailing list and stay updated").

Detox

The boxed wordOne word, tracked wide, in a lime box, like "TOTAL BELLY / D E T O X" on the homepage. Use it once per email.

Black Friday

Hot gradientDeep red to coral to orange, with yellow as the accent on top. Use it for heroes and closing bands, not whole emails.

Voice

Klaviyo describes THH's voice as approachable, encouraging, urgent and positive. Keep the warmth and the energy. Dial back the habits that make every email sound the same.

  • Keep: "Hey love," Shay in first person, real customer quotes, "Allow us to be your helping hand."
  • Dial back: an emoji on every line, "Hurry!" and "Limited stock" on every send, exclamation marks after every sentence.
  • Urgency only with a real date and time, stated once.
  • Sender name every time: Shay from The Healthy Hand.

Say this, not that

Supplements can make claims about how the body works, but not about disease or guaranteed weight loss.

Who gets what

Audience & guardrails

THH's Klaviyo only has suppression segments right now (bounces, can't receive, no email). These are the segments to create. The suppression segments stay excluded from every send.

Wave 1 · Engaged 180 days

Opened or clicked an email in the last 180 days, or placed an order in the last 365 days. Can receive email.

Wave 2 · Engaged 181–365 days

Opened or clicked in the last 365 days, and zero times in the last 180.

Wave 3 · Engaged 1–2 years

Opened or clicked in the last 730 days, and zero times in the last 365. Email 1 only, and only if waves 1 and 2 stay inside the guardrails.

Do not mail · Never engaged

No opens or clicks in 2 years. Leave them out of BFCM and run a sunset email in January.

BFCM26 · Early list

Clicked an email link containing early=bfcm26 since 11/17, or in the "VIP Customers List," or subscribed to SMS.

BFCM26 · Bought

Placed an order since Wed 11/25 at 6pm ET. Excluded from emails 5 and 6 and SMS 3 and 4.

Guardrails. After each wave of Email 1, check the spam complaint rate (keep it under 0.08%) and the bounce rate (under 1%). If either goes over, stop widening the list and send the sale only to waves already mailed.

Settings. Sender "Shay from The Healthy Hand" from info@thehealthyhand.com, with a reply inbox someone actually reads. Turn on UTM tracking (it was off for every send last year).

How we'll know it worked

Measure it

ReactivationShare of waves 2 and 3 who open or click Email 1. Early-list size by Wed 11/25.
RevenueTotal vs. last year's $745. Revenue per recipient and placed-order rate for each send.
List healthUnsubscribes under 0.3% per send, spam under 0.08%. Clicks well above last year's 0.5%.

A/B test: Email 4 subject lines, "Your Thanksgiving plate, itemized" vs. "We saw that second plate." Send each to 20% of the audience, pick the winner by click rate after 2 hours, then send it to the other 60%.

Before anything gets built

Needs sign-off

  1. The offer. The plan assumes 40% off everything, no code needed, Wed 11/25 6pm (early) to Mon 11/30 11:59pm ET. Confirm the percentage and whether bundles get it too, since they're already marked down.
  2. How early access works. Either schedule the Shopify automatic discount for Wed 6pm without announcing it publicly, or give the early list a Shopify discount link that applies 40% when they click.
  3. Shay reads Email 1. It's in her voice and mentions her daughter and the herbs she grew up with. She should change anything that isn't quite her.
  4. A photo of Shay. Her in the kitchen with a mug of tea, bright natural light. Email 1 is built around it.
  5. The site and packaging claims. The emails stay within the claim rules, but they link to product pages and show packaging that say "flush belly fat fast," "eliminate parasites," and that the tea lowers blood pressure and "prevents cancer." Those are the claims that get supplement brands FTC letters. Worth a review before BFCM traffic goes there.
  6. Small facts to check: tea steep time (we wrote 5 minutes), capsule directions (2 daily), the $100 free-shipping threshold, and holiday shipping times.
  7. Approve the brand kit. Once it's approved, it can be saved to Klaviyo's brand library, and the emails can be built as drag-and-drop templates.
